Purpose: to demonstrate to students that Cas9, under the guidance of proper sgRNAs, can cleave specific sites.

Method III (original, given up)
Dual plasmid:
  T=guided to ccdB
  NT=guided to GFP, which is absent in this system
    Plasmid1: sgRNA(T)-Cas9-AmpR
    Plasmid2: sgRNA(NT)-Cas9-AmpR
    Plasmid3: ccdB(IPTG induced)-KanR
Target=Plasmid1+3
NT=Plasmid2+3
Plate1=Amp+IPTG
Plate2=Amp+Kan+IPTG
Cultured on 2*2 plates respectively
Expected result:
  NT on plate 1 ×, on plate 2 ×
  T on plate 1 √, on plate 2 × (as Cas9 creates DSB on plasmid3 and plasmid3 is then digested by exonuclease)
Actual result:
  NT on plate 1 √ (probably because some colonies only comprise plasmid2, not 3, through this selection), on plate 2 ×
  T on plate 1 √, on plate 2 √ (probably because Cas9 cleavage efficiency wasn't 100%, or because KanR expressed product that can exist for a long time)
Conclusion:
  Too complex for educational experiment. Try to simplify it by getting rid of the selectivity that ccdB enacts, or to optimize it by introducing artificial HR systems, like λRed (which would involve a third plasmid that carries the HR donor and proves to be incredibly difficult: triple plasmid co-transformation??? not good)
